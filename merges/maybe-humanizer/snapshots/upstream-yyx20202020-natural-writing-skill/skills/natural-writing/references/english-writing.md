# English Writing

## High-impact symptoms

- Significance claims unsupported by a concrete consequence
- Promotional adjectives standing in for evidence
- Vague sources such as “experts say” or “research shows”
- Formulaic contrast, rule-of-three lists, and repeated section conclusions
- Nominalizations and weak copular constructions hiding the actor
- Meta-commentary that announces the next point
- Uniform sentence and paragraph rhythm
- Personality added where neutral reference prose is appropriate

## Rewrite moves

- State the claim before explaining why it matters.
- Name the actor, action, object, and evidence.
- Replace vague attribution with a source or remove the attribution.
- Use the smallest number of list items the content requires.
- Prefer strong verbs, but retain passive voice when the actor is irrelevant or unknown.
- Vary rhythm through thought structure, not random punctuation.
- Keep repeated terminology when consistency is more valuable than synonym variety.

## Strict mode

When the user asks for terse or hard-edged prose, apply stricter constraints to filler, abstraction, and repeated frames. Do not impose universal bans on adverbs, em dashes, passive voice, questions, or sentence fragments; judge whether each instance performs useful work.
