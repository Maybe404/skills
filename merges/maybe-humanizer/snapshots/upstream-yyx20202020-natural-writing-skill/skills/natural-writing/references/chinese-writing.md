# Chinese Writing

## High-impact symptoms

Focus on clusters, not isolated words.

### Empty framing

- Era-setting openings that add no necessary context
- Repeated transitions that announce structure instead of carrying meaning
- Conclusions that repeat the thesis with larger, vaguer nouns

### Performed significance

- Unsupported claims that something is crucial, transformative, profound, or unprecedented
- Product language that substitutes praise for evidence
- Abstract nouns such as “价值、能力、生态、闭环、赋能” without a concrete actor and action

### Mechanical syntax

- Repeated “不仅……更……” or “从……到……” frames
- Three-part lists created for rhythm rather than meaning
- Identical paragraph openings and summary endings
- Long modifier chains, translated passive constructions, and noun-heavy verbs

### False human texture

- Invented first-person experience
- Generic emotion inserted without evidence
- Forced rhetorical questions, internet slang, jokes, or “金句”
- Deliberate typos or disorder used as fake authenticity

## Rewrite moves

- Name the person or system performing the action.
- Replace category words with the actual event, object, constraint, or result.
- Let adjacent paragraphs connect through meaning rather than visible transition words.
- Vary rhythm only where it improves reading; do not randomize sentence length.
- Keep domain language when it is the clearest term.
- Prefer one supported point over three inflated points.

## Misfire prevention

Do not remove every occurrence of a suspected phrase. “关键” is valid when a key is literally required or evidence identifies a decisive factor. Repetition is valid in specifications. Passive voice is valid when the actor is unknown or intentionally out of scope.
